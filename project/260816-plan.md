# Whole-Balance-Sheet Research and Implementation Plan

## Mission

Determine the allocation across the family's complete net financial position that maximises expected after-tax return while:

- Preserving the ability to fund an Australian home purchase without borrowing
- Covering the household income deficit without forced sales
- Maintaining a suitable long-term growth portfolio
- Controlling currency, liquidity, duration, credit, equity, concentration, and tax risk

Do not optimise the AUD 2.2 million property requirement and the AUD 1.1 million Long-Term portfolio independently. Model them together first; assign functional labels after selecting the combined allocation.

This plan is a research and recommendation brief. Do not execute trades, transfers, FX conversions, or account openings without separate approval.

# 2. Critical research framing

## 2.1 Optimise the whole balance sheet

The central decision is not simply where to place AUD 2.2 million. It is:

> What combination of capital-stable, fixed-income, credit, equity, and real-asset exposure across AUD 3.322 million maximises expected after-tax return while keeping property-funding risk and household cash-flow risk acceptable?

The current portfolio already contains approximately AUD 1.1 million of equity and equity-like Long-Term assets. Any proposal to add growth assets to the property-funding pool must account for that existing exposure.

## 2.2 Meaning of “property-funding opportunity set”

This phrase does **not** mean investment property, residential-property securities, or bonds only.

It means every instrument that could contribute to funding the future home, including:

- Deposits
- Commonwealth and semi-government securities
- Investment-grade credit
- Inflation-linked securities
- Diversified equities
- Listed infrastructure and real assets
- Other liquid, transparent public instruments consistent with the constraints

Each instrument receives different recognition under stress. Liquidity alone does not make an asset capital-stable.

## 2.3 Separate cash income from total return

For every instrument and portfolio, report separately:

1. Gross cash income
2. After-tax cash income
3. Yield to maturity or expected total return
4. Taxable income
5. Cash-receipt timing
6. Principal movement
7. Stressed realisable value

Do not describe distribution yield, coupon yield, or trailing dividend yield as expected total return.

## 2.4 Avoid predetermined answers

Do not assume in advance that:

- Property funding must be 100% deposits and bonds
- It must contain equities
- The current Long-Term portfolio is optimal
- SOL or BHP must be sold
- SOL or BHP must be retained at any price
- A higher headline yield is better
- Existing functional labels dictate the final allocation

The research must compare alternatives and make trade-offs explicit.

For each of `JPST`, `FLBL`, and `CRED`, record:

- Currency exposure and cost of conversion or hedging
- Interest-rate, credit-spread, default, and liquidity stress
- Correlation with Property Capital and Long-Term equities

- Best replacement, if replacement is needed
- Final functional label and rationale

# Phase 6 — Stress and historical-window testing

## 6.1 Rate shocks

- Government yields +1%
- Government yields +1.5%
- Government yields +2%
- Government yields −1%
- Real yields +1%

## 6.2 Credit shocks

- Credit spreads +1%
- Credit spreads +2%
- Credit spreads +3%
- Default/loss assumptions appropriate to any lower-quality exposure

## 6.3 Equity and real-asset shocks

- Global equities −20%, −35%, and −50%
- Australian equities −35%
- Listed infrastructure and REITs stressed using observed equity-like behavior
- Equity distributions reduced
- BHP dividend materially reduced or suspended in the severe case

## 6.4 Combined stress

At minimum:

- Government yields +1.5%
- Credit spreads +2%
- Global/Australian equities −35%
- No favorable FX movement
- Reduced equity distributions
- Normal sale costs and wider stressed spreads

## 6.5 Purchase dates

Assume the full AUD 2.2 million is required in:

- August 2027
- August 2028
- August 2029
- August 2030

Calculate realisable AUD proceeds after spreads, tax, settlement timing, and break costs—not statement value alone.

## 6.6 Historical rolling windows

For market-risk allocations, test historical rolling holding periods of:

- 1 year
- 2 years
- 3 years
- 4 years

Report:

- Frequency of loss
- Worst result
- Median result
- Recovery time
- Frequency that property-funding proceeds fall below AUD 2.2m

Do not present historical frequency as a guaranteed future probability.

## 6.7 Acceptance criteria

A recommended portfolio must:

- Keep Operating Capital intact under the base stress
- Require no borrowing
- Show explicitly how AUD 2.2m becomes available at each purchase date
- Quantify any stressed funding shortfall rather than hiding it through labels
- Keep any accepted shortfall within a consciously approved risk budget
- Avoid dependence on one issuer, currency, or macro forecast
- Demonstrate that incremental expected return compensates for incremental shortfall risk

# Research quality standard

Every conclusion must distinguish:

- Fact from assumption
- Current quote from historical observation
- Cash yield from total return
- Liquidity from price stability
- Expected return from guaranteed return
- Tax estimate from tax filing advice
- Instrument-level risk from whole-balance-sheet impact

Use primary sources where available. Preserve source URLs, access dates, calculations, and raw data so another reviewer can reproduce the result.
