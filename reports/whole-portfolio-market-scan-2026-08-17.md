# Whole-Portfolio Market Scan — Phase 2 Opportunity Set

**Date:** 17 August 2026  
**Market-data window:** 12–16 August 2026 for rates/yields; 17 August 2026 for Lane 2G ETF history  
**Purpose:** Research the property-funding opportunity set without presuming a bond-only portfolio  
**Status:** Opportunity-set research; not an implementation recommendation or personal tax advice

## Executive conclusion

The opportunity set supports a **liability-first AUD portfolio**, but does not justify chasing the highest advertised yield:

1. **Operating Capital and near-term property cash** should use verified at-call deposits, short term deposits, and maturity-matched Commonwealth securities.
2. **Commonwealth bonds** provide the cleanest maturity control. The 2027–2030 curve is approximately 4.55–4.61% YTM; GSBG27 and GSBG33 are confirmed holdings with known maturity dates.
3. **Term deposits** offer a meaningful gross premium, approximately 0.6–0.8 percentage points over comparable Commonwealth yields, but exchange liquidity for FCS diversification, break restrictions, and reinvestment risk.
4. **Semi-government bonds** remain a candidate sleeve, but this scan does not contain executable line-level prices. The current spread estimates are not decision-grade.
5. **Credit** can improve income but does not provide a free yield pickup. CRED is capped at 15% and remains subject to factsheet confirmation and spread-stress testing.
6. **Indexed bonds** are a valid 0–5% inflation-protection option, not a required allocation. Their real yields are attractive, but duration, tax timing, and liquidity reduce their suitability for all property capital.
7. **Lane 2G confirms that growth and real assets are materially different risk assets.** Global equities, REITs, infrastructure, gold, and growth multi-asset funds can improve long-run expected return, but a 35–50% equity-style stress can impair the property purchase plan. They should not enter Property Capital without an explicit combined-balance-sheet decision.

The Phase 1 cash-income hurdle remains below the 3.5% planning floor. Therefore, Phase 2 does not need to maximize yield; it needs to identify the lowest-risk instruments capable of funding the liability on the required dates.

### Decision test

The gross TD premium over 2027–2030 Commonwealth YTM is approximately **0.6–0.8 percentage points** in the observed scan. After tax, that is approximately 0.36–0.48 percentage points before considering the value of liquidity, break penalties, FCS diversification, and reinvestment risk. This is potentially worthwhile for a defined maturity tranche, but not for capital that may be needed on 30 days' notice.

### Shortlist constraints

- Ladder only across distinct ADIs; MoveBank is linked to Regional Australia Bank and must not be counted as an independent licence from that ADI.
- Keep each ADI's aggregate protected balance at or below AUD 250k per account holder.
- Place maturity dates before or around the possible property purchase dates, not solely at the highest quoted term.
- Obtain written maturity instructions and rollover defaults before funding.

### Cash versus total return

GSBG27's 4.75% coupon exceeds its 4.586% YTM because it trades at a premium. The coupon is cash income; the premium amortisation is part of the economic total-return calculation. Its recalculated modified duration is 0.650 years, not 0.49 years. Future purchase-date value must use dirty price, accrued interest, executable YTM, brokerage, and spread—not coupon yield alone.

### Purchase-date testing requirement

For each candidate bond, Phase 6 must calculate realized proceeds at August 2027, August 2028, August 2029, and August 2030 under the applicable yield shock. A statement value is not sufficient. The corrected duration series is based on settlement 17 August 2026, semiannual cash flows, actual coupon dates, and dirty-price/YTM discounting. Indexed durations are real-duration approximations that exclude indexation lag and accrued indexation; they must be refined from executable indexed-bond terms before stress testing.

### Lane 2G interpretation

- A 35% and 50% equity stress is plausible for the equity, REIT, infrastructure, and growth multi-asset candidates; it is not a remote theoretical event.
- Listed REITs and infrastructure are not substitutes for cash or government bonds. They can be rate-sensitive, equity-sensitive, and correlated with the same macro shock that affects property affordability.
- Gold has no contractual cash yield and should be evaluated as a diversifier, not as income capital.
- Hedged versus unhedged global equity changes AUD volatility; neither is a reliable hedge for Australian residential-property prices.
- Residential-property correlation is **not assumed**. No candidate receives hedge credit merely because it is labeled real asset or infrastructure.

## Cross-lane opportunity-set ranking

### Core property-funding shortlist

1. Commonwealth securities maturing 2027–2030, especially direct maturity-matched lines.
2. Unconditional at-call deposits for Operating Capital and immediate liquidity.
3. FCS-diversified term deposits for capital with a defined maturity date.
4. Select semi-government lines, conditional on executable spreads of sufficient size.
5. A small indexed-bond sleeve, 0–5%, only if the 2030 real-yield/liquidity test passes.
6. CRED only as a capped satellite sleeve after factsheet and stress confirmation.

### Deliberately separate / do not admit by label

- VGS, VGAD, VAS, AQLT, HQLT, MVW, IFRA, VAP, GOLD, DHHF, and QUAL remain Lane 2G growth/real-asset candidates for Long-Term or combined-balance-sheet testing.
- They should not be counted as Property Capital income merely because they distribute cash.
- No growth or real-asset candidate receives a property-liability hedge assumption.
