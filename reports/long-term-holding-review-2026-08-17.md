## Executive conclusion

The Long-Term sleeve is approximately **AUD 1,106,728** at the 14 August 2026 market snapshot. It is already a mixed portfolio, but its dominant risks are not evenly diversified:

- Australian individual equities and the Australian market ETF are a large overlapping block.
- `SOL.AX` is a diversified listed investment company, but at roughly 26% of the sleeve it is still a large single-security exposure.
- `BHP.AX`, `AGL.AX`, and `ORG.AX` add commodity, China, domestic-demand, and energy-cycle exposure that is not visible from dividend income alone.
- `VGS.AX` is the main developed-market diversifier; `VGE.AX` is a separate emerging-markets risk, not a substitute for developed markets.
- `UKWl` is a listed renewable-infrastructure exposure with GBP economic exposure and meaningful interest-rate, power-price, and project-risk sensitivity.
- `GHY.AX` is a small speculative/pre-revenue position and produces no current income.
- `CRED.AX`, `FLBL`, and `JPST` are not treated as core Long-Term equity exposures here. The Phase 1 baseline assigns CRED to Property Capital and FLBL/JPST to transition because their credit and/or USD exposure does not match the AUD property liability.

The strongest strategic observation is whole-balance-sheet rather than security-specific: Property Capital and Operating Capital already supply substantial defensive capacity. If the long-term sleeve can remain untouched for 20+ years, a high-equity, globally diversified structure is defensible. The main unresolved decision is how much Australian income and existing embedded tax gain is worth retaining in exchange for lower concentration and broader global exposure.

## Mandate and assumptions

- Horizon: 20+ years.
- No planned withdrawals while Operating Capital remains adequate.
- Objective: maximise after-tax real total return, after fees, tax, and transaction costs.
- Income is useful but subordinate to total return and thesis quality.
- Equity dividends are variable and are not treated as contractual household income.
- The acceptable Long-Term drawdown assumption for this phase is **45% peak-to-trough on the sleeve** in a severe global equity event, provided Property and Operating Capital remain ring-fenced and no sale is required. This is a planning assumption, not a forecast; Phase 6 must test it.
- Planning tax assumptions are inherited from the Phase 1 report: Australian resident individual owner, no employment income in the owner's name, 100% franking assumed for SOL/BHP/VAS/AGL/ORG, 30% foreign-tax gross-up for VGS/VGE, and approximately AUD 50k carried-forward capital losses. These need confirmation from tax records and AMMA/dividend statements.
- Expected-return ranges below are **nominal, forward planning ranges**, not predictions. They are deliberately wide and are not historical-return extrapolations.

## Aggregate exposure assessment

The database classifications and security look-through should be refreshed before implementation. On the current holdings, the following qualitative exposures are clear:

- **Australian risk assets:** SOL, BHP, VAS, AGL, ORG, and the AUD-listed infrastructure fund. VAS overlaps the individual Australian holdings, especially resources and energy. The overlap is not necessarily wrong, but it means the apparent number of positions overstates diversification.
- **Commodity / China:** BHP is direct; VAS and SOL add indirect Australian resource exposure. BHP income must not be used as a stability proxy.
- **Energy:** AGL and ORG are direct; SOL and VAS add indirect exposure. UKWl is an energy-transition/real-asset exposure but has different operating and currency risks.
- **Developed markets:** VGS is the cleanest broad exposure. SOL is not a substitute because its look-through portfolio, domicile, and manager exposures differ from a market-cap global ETF.
- **Emerging markets:** VGE is a deliberate separate sleeve. It should be measured independently from VGS, not treated as developed-market exposure.
- **Currency:** AUD is dominant through Australian assets; VGS/VGE/UKWl provide unhedged foreign-currency exposure. The acceptable amount depends on whole-balance-sheet AUD liabilities and should be tested in Phase 6.
- **Equity beta:** Most of the Long-Term sleeve is equity-like. CRED/FLBL/JPST should not be used to disguise this risk because credit spreads can correlate with equities in stress.

## Correlation and valuation context

The local DB does not currently contain a usable multi-year price history for the August snapshot: the available `prices` rows are concentrated around 14–16 August 2026. A numeric volatility, maximum-drawdown, or correlation estimate from this database would therefore be false precision. Phase 6 should populate a clean total-return series, including distributions where available, before selecting among alternatives.

Valuation is likewise not reduced to a single current P/E or discount number in this review. For broad ETFs, valuation is an aggregate market property; for SOL, UKWl, and the operating companies it depends on NAV, earnings, debt, and asset-specific assumptions. The correct use of valuation here is a sensitivity input, not a timing signal. A holding should not be retained solely because it has fallen, nor sold solely because it has risen.
