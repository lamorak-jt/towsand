## Executive summary

All six research lanes are covered. The yield environment is declining — the RBA delivered three rate cuts in 2025 — so current rates represent a window that may not persist. Key findings:

1. **At-call deposits** offer up to 6.00% (ING, with conditions) and 4.50–5.00% unconditional. All FCS-covered to AUD 250k per ADI.
2. **Term deposits** offer 5.05–5.55% across 3-month to 5-year terms, with the best rates from smaller ADIs (Judo Bank, MoveBank, Unity Bank).
3. **Commonwealth government bonds** yield 4.40–5.55% across the curve, with GSBG27 (4.75% Apr-2027) at 4.586% and GSBG33 (4.50% Apr-2033) at 4.769%. **GSBG27 coupon confirmed as 4.75% from AOFM** — correcting the Phase 1 working assumption of 4.50%.
4. **Semi-government bonds** were not directly retrievable (RBA F16.1 URLs returned 404). Estimated spreads of 10–20bps over equivalent Commonwealth maturities based on market knowledge. **This lane needs broker confirmation.**
5. **Investment-grade credit** via CRED.AX offers a distribution yield of ~5.32% with an estimated duration of ~4 years. The fund is capped at 15% of Property Capital per the plan.
6. **Inflation-linked bonds** offer real yields of 2.62–2.87% across 2027–2040 maturities. Break-even inflation is ~2.0%, below the RBA target band of 2–3%, suggesting indexed bonds may offer value if inflation exceeds 2%.

### Key observations

1. **The best 12-month rate (5.40%, MoveBank) exceeds the best government bond yield at comparable maturity** (GSBG27 at 4.586%, ~8 months). This is because TDs carry bank credit risk (mitigated by FCS) and lack the capital gain potential of bonds.

2. **The term deposit curve is relatively flat** at 5.25–5.55% from 11 months to 5 years, with a slight peak at 2 years (5.55%). This contrasts with the government bond curve, which slopes upward from 4.55% (2yr) to 5.55% (30yr).

3. **Break penalties are significant.** Early withdrawal requires 31 days' notice and incurs an interest rate reduction of 20–90% depending on the proportion of term elapsed. This reduces flexibility.

4. **FCS coverage is AUD 250k per ADI per account holder.** A term deposit ladder using distinct ADIs is needed for amounts above AUD 250k per institution.

5. **Interest is typically simple, not compound.** At-maturity TDs do not reinvest interest during the term. For income-matching purposes (funding the household deficit), this is acceptable — interest is received as cash.

### Decision question

> Does the term deposit offer enough additional after-tax return over at-call deposits or Commonwealth securities to compensate for reduced flexibility?

**Answer:** For terms of 6–18 months, TDs at 5.25–5.40% offer ~0.45–0.80% over equivalent Commonwealth bonds (~4.55–4.60%). Given FCS coverage, this spread is attractive for the deposit sleeve. For longer terms (3–5 years), TDs at 5.40–5.55% offer ~0.60–0.80% over equivalent bonds (~4.55–4.77%), but lock away capital with break penalties.

### Break-even inflation analysis

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Nominal 10yr yield | 4.641% |
| Real 10yr yield | 2.633% |
| Break-even inflation | 2.008% |
| RBA inflation target | 2–3% (midpoint 2.5%) |

### Decision question

> Does the current real yield justify the duration risk, and does the instrument improve protection against property-cost growth?

**Analysis:**

1. **Real yields of 2.6–2.9% are attractive in historical context.** They provide a guaranteed real return above inflation, which is valuable for matching property costs that will inflate over time.

2. **Break-even inflation of ~2.0% is below the RBA target midpoint of 2.5%.** If inflation averages 2.5% over the holding period, indexed bonds will outperform nominal bonds by ~0.5% per annum.

3. **Duration risk is significant.** The 2030 indexed bond has a real duration of ~4 years, and the 2035 bond has ~9 years. In a rising real yield environment, the price impact could be material.

4. **Tax treatment of indexation is complex.** The principal indexation is taxed as a capital gain on sale (eligible for the 50% CGT discount if held >12 months). This makes indexed bonds more tax-efficient than nominal bonds for the principal growth component, but less tax-efficient for the coupon component (which is a low fixed rate).

5. **Liquidity is lower than nominal bonds.** Indexed bonds have smaller outstanding amounts and lower trading volumes.

### Output — recommendation

**Recommend 0–5% allocation to indexed bonds.** The lower end of the plan's 0–10% range is appropriate because:

- The duration risk is significant for maturities beyond 2030
- The tax treatment of indexation adds complexity
- Liquidity is lower than nominal bonds
- The break-even inflation of 2.0% offers only a modest margin over the RBA target

If included, the 2030 indexed bond (2.50% coupon, 2.662% real yield, ~4yr duration) is the best fit for Property Capital's 2027–2030 purchase window. A AUD 100k–150k allocation would provide inflation protection without excessive duration risk.

## Appendix B — RBA F16.1 semi-government data gap

The RBA F16.1 table (Indicative Mid Rates of Selected Australian Semi-Government Securities) was listed on the RBA statistics tables page, but both CSV (`f16-1-data.csv`) and XLSX (`f16-1-data.xlsx`) URL patterns returned 404 errors. The RBA may have restructured the URL pattern or made the data available only through the HTML sub-pages (F16.1 – NSW, F16.1 – VIC, etc.). Semi-government yields should be obtained from:

1. Broker quotes at execution time
2. ASX eAGB system (if accessible)
3. Financial data providers (Bloomberg, Refinitiv)
4. RBA F16.1 HTML pages (if fetchable with correct URLs)

Typical semi-government spreads over Commonwealth bonds are 5–25 bps depending on issuer and maturity. These estimates have been used in the Lane D analysis above.
