# Long-Term Allocation Recommendation

**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Plan ref:** `project/260816-plan.md`, Phase 3  
**Status:** Research recommendation for Phase 5; **not a trade instruction**

## Decision

Carry **LT-B — Globally diversified** and **LT-E — Tax-minimal transition** into Phase 5 combined whole-balance-sheet construction. Do not select a final portfolio or recommend trades until Phase 4 confirms the treatment of FLBL/JPST/UKWl and the tax-cost schedule is complete.

- **LT-B** is the cleanest strategic benchmark: it reduces Australian and single-security concentration while retaining deliberate AUD, franked-income, emerging-market, and real-asset sleeves.
- **LT-E** is the practical implementation benchmark: it recognises that an apparently better allocation can be worse after CGT, spread, FX, and transaction costs.
- **LT-A** is a valid status-quo comparator but is not a preferred strategic endpoint because it retains too much concentrated Australian and energy/commodity exposure and includes transitional credit/cash assets in the Long-Term mandate.
- **LT-C** is appropriate only if income preference is elevated above the stated total-return objective. Its income is variable and does not eliminate the Property Capital requirement.
- **LT-D** is the return-maximising comparator, not the default: it has the greatest estimated drawdown and lowest cash income and needs explicit whole-balance-sheet drawdown acceptance.

The after-tax ranges are deliberately approximate. Australian franking credits, foreign tax offsets, AMMA components, carried-forward losses, Medicare/MLS, and the owner's other taxable income can move the result materially. The Phase 1 report correctly warns that Long-Term income cannot be added mechanically to Property Capital income under progressive taxation.

## Why LT-B is the strategic benchmark

1. `VGS` becomes the core rather than one of several Australian-heavy holdings.
2. `VAS` remains large enough to preserve AUD exposure and possible franking value, but no longer duplicates the full Australian market plus several individual Australian names at the same scale.
3. `VGE` is labelled honestly as emerging-market risk.
4. `SOL`, `BHP`, and `UKWl` remain intentional satellites rather than accidental portfolio anchors.
5. Credit is limited and explicitly separated from equity beta.
6. The structure is easier to stress-test and explain at whole-balance-sheet level.

## Why LT-E must remain alongside LT-B

The IB statements already provide substantial evidence for the tax-cost comparison: all SOL purchase dates and cost bases, recent BHP lots with the long-term/short-term split, aggregate AUD cost bases for foreign positions, realised and unrealised performance, and loss-position evidence for FLBL and UKWl. The database export is not yet a complete parcel-level tax ledger, and some older lot detail, ownership attribution, discount eligibility, and loss reconciliation remain to be verified. Phase 4 is therefore a tractable reconciliation and scenario exercise—not a tax analysis starting from zero. LT-E remains necessary as a guard against turning a strategically better allocation into a worse after-tax transaction.

Implementation principles for LT-E:

- Use new Long-Term capital first where available.
- Direct maturities and new distributions to the target global exposure.
- Use carried-forward capital losses against eligible realised gains only after confirming the tax schedule.
- Prefer parcels with losses, small gains, or clear CGT discount eligibility when reducing concentration.
- Do not sell UKWl solely because its current AUD mark is below recorded cost; assess thesis, tax, and portfolio role together.
- Do not count FLBL or JPST as Property Capital until AUD conversion, liquidity, withholding, FX, and stress treatment are confirmed.

## Property Capital income requirement

The Phase 1 tax baseline estimates a Property Capital gross cash-income breakeven of approximately **2.24–3.23%**, depending on franking and defensive-income assumptions, against the AUD 70,596 annual household deficit. Long-Term distributions can reduce the amount drawn from Operating Capital, but they are not a substitute for the Property Capital mandate because equity distributions can be cut during the same stress in which employment or asset prices deteriorate.

At the indicative gross income levels above, the Long-Term sleeve may provide useful supplementary cash, but no alternative fully covers the household deficit on its own. The allocation decision should therefore be based primarily on after-tax real total return and forced-sale avoidance, not on maximising the Long-Term distribution rate.

## Required Phase 4/5 work before final selection

1. Reconcile the IB evidence into parcel-level LT-B and LT-E tax schedules, filling only the remaining older-lot, ownership, discount-eligibility, and loss-reconciliation gaps.
2. Verify VAS/VGS/VGE AMMA components and the actual franking of Australian company holdings.
3. Confirm UKWl debt, NAV discount, distribution cover, and GBP exposure while preserving its loss-position evidence in the comparison.
4. Decide whether CRED belongs in Property Capital or Long-Term after the Phase 2 credit analysis.
5. Replace qualitative drawdown ranges with a clean total-return history and the Phase 6 stress windows.
6. Test each candidate combined portfolio against the property purchase window, not just against Long-Term statistics.

**Conclusion:** LT-B is the strategic reference portfolio; LT-E is the likely implementation path. No trade is recommended in this Phase 3 report.
