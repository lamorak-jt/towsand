# Portfolio Management Rules

## One-Sentence Summary

Assign capital first to the AUD property requirement and household operating reserve, then invest the genuine long-term surplus for real growth without allowing market, currency, liquidity, or income risk to impair either near-term function.

---

## 0. Capital Definitions

### 0.1 Net Financial Position

Net financial position includes:

- Instrument holdings at current AUD market value
- Cash and deposit balances at current AUD value
- Financial liabilities at current AUD value

Receivables with uncertain timing or recovery are excluded until received. Liabilities are never excluded from balance-sheet adequacy merely because they are not investment positions.

### 0.2 Functional Assignment

Every financial asset is assigned to one primary function:

1. **Property Capital**
2. **Operating Capital**
3. **Long-Term Capital**

An asset may not satisfy two functional requirements simultaneously.

Account location does not determine function. Cash, deposits, and securities are classified according to their intended use.

### 0.3 Long-Term Investable Assets

Percentage-based role, position, concentration, macro, and growth-currency rules use **Long-Term Capital** as their denominator unless a rule explicitly states otherwise.

Property Capital and Operating Capital are excluded from that denominator. This prevents capital reserved for known liabilities from creating false allocation breaches or recommendations to deploy it into growth assets.

### 0.4 Instrument Universe

The portfolio is restricted to:

- Long-only positions
- Non-leveraged instruments
- Non-derivative instruments
- Publicly listed or deposit-based instruments

No margin, short selling, futures, options, warrants, leveraged ETFs, structured products, private funds, or illiquid alternatives are permitted.

## 1. Property Capital Rules

### 1.1 Target

Property Capital must have a current designated value of at least **AUD 2,200,000**.

The target represents the complete expected cash requirement. It is reviewed under Rule 1.10 but is not automatically increased by a generic contingency percentage.

### 1.2 Currency

Property Capital must have **100% AUD economic exposure**.

Unhedged foreign-currency assets do not count toward the target. Foreign cash without a matching foreign liability is converted to AUD before assignment to Property Capital.

### 1.3 Eligible Assets

Core Property Capital may include:

- AUD at-call deposits
- AUD term deposits
- Commonwealth government securities
- State government and other high-grade AUD fixed-income securities
- Diversified short-duration AUD investment-grade credit
- A limited allocation to intermediate-duration or inflation-linked AUD securities

The following do not count as core Property Capital:

- Equities
- Speculative assets
- Unhedged foreign-currency assets
- Lower-quality or materially credit-sensitive instruments
- Assets whose disposal depends on an illiquid private market

### 1.4 Strategic Allocation Before Active Search

The starting mandate is:

| Sleeve | AUD | Allocation |
|---|---:|---:|
| At-call FCS-covered deposits | 200,000 | 9% |
| Term deposits maturing before or around August 2027 | 400,000 | 18% |
| Short Commonwealth government ladder | 600,000 | 27% |
| Semi-government and comparable AAA-quality AUD ladder | 400,000 | 18% |
| Short-duration investment-grade AUD credit | 300,000 | 14% |
| Intermediate Commonwealth government bonds | 200,000 | 9% |
| Inflation-linked government securities | 100,000 | 5% |
| **Total** | **2,200,000** | **100%** |

Permitted construction ranges are:

- **Deposits and near-cash:** 20–35%
- **Commonwealth, semi-government, and comparable AAA-quality securities:** 50–70%
- **Investment-grade credit:** 5–15%
- **Intermediate duration above four years:** maximum 10%
- **Inflation-linked government securities:** 0–10%

The model is not a requirement to buy an unattractive sleeve. Eligible instruments compete on after-tax yield to worst after fees, stress loss, liquidity, maturity, and counterparty protection. Credit is not increased merely to reach an income target.

### 1.5 Timing and Liquidity Glide Path

The property cannot be required before August 2027. From August 2027, purchase probability increases through an approximate three-year window.

| Stage | Near-cash or ≤1-year | Short/intermediate government | Credit/other |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before August 2027 | 25–35% | 50–60% | 10–15% |
| August 2027–August 2028 | 30–40% | 50–60% | 10–15% |
| August 2028–August 2029 | 40–55% | 35–50% | 5–10% |
| August 2029–August 2030 | 60–75% | 20–35% | 0–5% |
| Purchase imminent | 80–100% | 0–20% | 0% |

Calendar bands are subordinate to the event triggers in Rule 1.6.

### 1.6 Property Event Triggers

**Purchase plausible within 12 months**

- Stop adding duration.
- Reduce credit exposure.
- Direct coupons, maturities, and new capital toward near-cash assets.

**Active property search or regular inspections**

- Hold at least 50–70% settlement-ready.
- Restrict the remainder to highly liquid government securities capable of being sold within the expected transaction timetable.

**Preparing to make offers**

- Make the full AUD 2.2 million settlement-ready.
- Do not rely on equities, foreign currency, credit-spread recovery, or term deposits that cannot be broken reliably.

### 1.7 Duration

Duration risk is budgeted rather than prohibited.

- Weighted duration should normally remain within approximately two to three years before active search.
- No more than 10% of Property Capital may have duration above four years.
- A maturity matched to a known pre-purchase date is assessed by expected maturity proceeds rather than interim price volatility.
- Duration must fall as purchase probability rises.

### 1.8 Stress Requirement

Property Capital is tested for:

- A 1.5 percentage-point parallel increase in government yields
- A simultaneous 2 percentage-point widening in investment-grade credit spreads
- Normal sale costs and bid/ask spreads
- No favourable equity or foreign-exchange movement

After stress:

- The realisable value should remain at least 95% of the current property target.
- Any residual shortfall must be coverable from other liquid, non-equity assets without borrowing or forced equity sales.
- A failed stress test requires reduced duration, reduced credit exposure, or additional designated capital.

### 1.9 Counterparty and Deposit Protection

- Deposit exposure is assessed per account holder and authorized deposit-taking institution, not per branded account.
- Deposit concentration should account for the Australian Financial Claims Scheme limit and shared ADI licences.
- Amounts above applicable protection limits require an explicit yield, operational, or diversification justification.
- Large balances must not remain at a payment provider or broker solely for convenience when a safer or better-returning eligible destination is available.

### 1.10 Target Review

The complete property requirement is reviewed:

- Every six months from August 2027
- When the intended location or property type becomes more specific
- When active searching begins
- When evidence indicates a material change in purchase price or transaction costs

A changed target immediately changes the amount available as Long-Term Capital.

### 1.11 Selection by After-Tax Return

Eligible instruments are compared using:

- After-tax yield to maturity or yield to worst
- Fees, spreads, and transaction costs
- Duration and credit stress loss
- Maturity and reinvestment risk
- Access within the required timetable
- Sovereign, deposit-guarantee, and counterparty protection

The selection rule is:

**after-tax yield to worst − fees − expected stress loss − liquidity penalty**

Use a protected deposit when it offers better compensation than a comparable marketable security. Use government or semi-government securities when their liquidity, maturity, or yield is superior. Reject additional credit or duration when its incremental yield does not compensate for stress risk.

Headline yield alone is not a sufficient selection criterion.

### 1.12 Property Income Objective

The current annual household income deficit is **AUD 70,596**. Property Capital would need an after-tax cash return of **3.21%** to cover that deficit by itself.

The indicative gross yield hurdle is approximately **4.0–4.8%**, depending on the effective tax rate and the character of returns. This is an objective within the Property Capital risk constraints, not a mandatory yield target.

If eligible low-risk assets do not meet the hurdle:

- Do not add equity, foreign-exchange, lower-quality credit, or excessive duration risk to Property Capital.
- Apply the household income waterfall in Rule 2.5.
- Use Operating Capital for the residual deficit.

Income used for household expenditure does not increase the designated Property Capital value. Unused income is reinvested.

## 2. Operating Capital Rules

### 2.1 Current Requirement

Operating Capital must cover at least 18 months of the current secure-income deficit.

Current assumptions:

- Monthly core expenditure: **AUD 11,383**
- Secure monthly net income: **AUD 5,500**
- Monthly deficit: **AUD 5,883**
- Required Operating Capital: **AUD 105,894**

### 2.2 Eligible Assets

Operating Capital must be:

- AUD-denominated
- Accessible without depending on favourable market conditions
- Held in deposits or very short, high-quality instruments

Operating Capital is separate from Property Capital even when both are held in the same account or instrument type.

### 2.3 Recalculation Triggers

Recalculate the requirement when:

- Secure income changes by more than 10%
- Core expenditure changes by more than 10%
- Forward income visibility falls below six months
- A new recurring expense or income source becomes contractually visible

Uncontracted startup income is assigned a value of zero.

### 2.4 Income Shock Action

If secure income falls materially or visibility falls below six months:

- Freeze new optionality deployment.
- Recalculate the 18-month deficit requirement immediately.
- Refill Operating Capital before adding to Long-Term Capital.
- Review the Property Capital stress buffer for any reliance on household cash flow.

### 2.5 Household Income Waterfall

Fund household expenditure in this order:

1. Secure employment income
2. Reliable Property Capital interest and coupons
3. Long-Term Capital dividends and distributions
4. Operating Capital for the residual deficit

Equity distributions are supplementary and are not assumed to remain constant. Property Capital income not required for expenditure is reinvested into Property Capital.

## 3. Long-Term Capital Role Rules

### 3.1 Role Allocation Bands

Role bands apply only to Long-Term Capital:

- **Stabiliser:** 0–10%
- **Compounder:** 90–100%, including any Optionality allocation
- **Optionality:** 0–2%

Property and Operating Capital already provide substantial balance-sheet defensiveness. Long-Term Stabiliser Capital is therefore optional rather than mandatory. Optionality has no minimum allocation. Cash is classified by assigned function; it is not automatically treated as Long-Term Stabiliser Capital.

### 3.2 Priority

Property and Operating requirements take priority over all Long-Term role bands. A reduction in Long-Term Capital caused by a higher property or operating requirement is not itself a role-band breach, but the residual Long-Term portfolio must be recalculated.

### 3.3 Breach Action

A role breach caused by a deliberate reassignment of capital must be reviewed within 30 days. Market drift below 10% relative to the applicable limit does not require immediate trading if remediation would be tax-inefficient or costly.

## 4. Long-Term Position Size Rules

### 4.1 Single Position Caps

Position sizing is assessed against both Long-Term Capital and the total net financial position:

- Single listed operating company: maximum 30% of Long-Term Capital and 10% of net financial position
- Single diversified listed investment company, ETF, or fund: maximum 30% of Long-Term Capital and 15% of net financial position
- Single corporate credit issuer: maximum 7% of Long-Term Capital
- Speculative or pre-revenue asset: maximum 1% of Long-Term Capital
- Speculative assets in aggregate: maximum 3% of Long-Term Capital

A position within these limits is not automatically increased to the cap. Internal diversification, look-through factor exposure, thesis quality, downside to the whole family plan, tax costs, and correlation with other holdings determine the appropriate size.

A diversified investment company or fund is not treated as equivalent to a single operating business, but its underlying factor and issuer concentrations remain subject to Rules 4.2 and 5.

### 4.2 Issuer and Economic Concentration

- No more than 20% may be exposed to one corporate group.
- Government issuers held within Property or Operating Capital are assessed under those functions rather than this corporate concentration rule.
- Look-through exposure is used where reliable fund data is available.

## 5. Long-Term Macro and Correlation Rules

### 5.1 Australia Concentration

AUD-domiciled risk assets must not exceed 55% of Long-Term Capital. AUD government and operating-reserve assets are excluded from this risk-asset calculation.

### 5.2 Single Macro Driver

Exposure to any one of the following must not exceed 30% of Long-Term Capital:

- Australian housing outside the planned primary residence
- Bulk commodities
- China demand
- Global credit spreads

### 5.3 Stress Correlation

Assets expected to have greater than 0.7 correlation in stress are treated as one risk cluster for sizing. Fund diversification does not override common factor exposure.

---

## 6. Long-Term Currency Rules

### 6.1 Growth Capital Bands

For Long-Term Compounder and Optionality Capital:

- AUD economic exposure: 40–70%
- Non-AUD economic exposure: 30–60%

Currency is classified by economic exposure, not listing currency.

### 6.2 International Exposure

At least 40% of international growth exposure must remain unhedged. Hedging may be used only through non-derivative instruments consistent with Rule 0.4.

Property and Operating Capital are excluded because they require AUD liability matching.

---

## 7. Optionality Rules

### 7.1 Payoff Test

Optionality must satisfy at least two of:

- Portfolio downside explicitly bounded by position size
- Non-linear or disproportionately large upside in the identified favourable regime
- Expected stress-period outperformance relative to ordinary equity beta

### 7.2 Yield Exclusion

Yield, carry, or spread compression alone does not qualify as optionality. Optionality is not purchased merely to meet an allocation target.

---

## 8. Long-Term Stabiliser Rules

### 8.1 Purpose

Long-Term Stabiliser Capital controls drawdown within the long-term portfolio. It is not a substitute for Property or Operating Capital.

### 8.2 Liquidity

At least 70% must be liquid within five trading days at ordinary spread cost.

### 8.3 Duration and Inflation

- No single duration point may exceed 40% of Long-Term Stabiliser Capital.
- Inflation-linked or real-rate-sensitive exposure is evaluated across both Property and Long-Term Stabiliser Capital.
- Inflation protection is selected when its after-tax expected return and stress behavior improve the combined balance sheet; there is no mechanical requirement to hold an unsuitable instrument merely to reach a percentage.

---

## 9. Drawdown Rules

### 9.1 Property Independence

A 35% equity-market drawdown must not prevent the property purchase under the current Property Capital stress framework.

### 9.2 Operating Independence

The 18-month operating requirement must not depend on selling equities or materially credit-sensitive assets.

### 9.3 No Forced Sale

No modeled stress may require liquidation of Long-Term Compounder assets to fund ordinary household expenditure. A modest Property Capital stress shortfall may use other liquid non-equity assets only as permitted by Rule 1.8.

---

## 10. Review and Enforcement

### 10.1 Review Triggers

Review the relevant capital function when:

- Secure income or expenditure changes materially
- Property timing, search activity, target location, or expected complete cost changes
- The property glide path enters a new stage
- Relative after-tax returns among eligible low-risk instruments change materially
- Inflation, rates, currency conditions, or credit spreads shift structurally
- Stress correlations converge
- A functional capital boundary is breached
- A rule breach persists beyond its remediation period

### 10.2 Permitted Portfolio Maintenance

Without a strategic trigger, transactions are still permitted for:

- Maturity and liquidity management
- Maintaining the Property Capital glide path
- Restoring functional reserve adequacy
- Tax efficiency
- Lower fees or spreads
- Improved after-tax yield at equal or lower risk
- Corporate actions and instrument closure

Discretionary trading based solely on short-term market forecasts is not permitted.

### 10.3 Decision Record

Material reassignment between Property, Operating, and Long-Term Capital must record:

- Amount reassigned
- Trigger
- Effect on each capital requirement
- Tax and transaction-cost implications
- Post-change stress result
